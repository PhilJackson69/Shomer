## Legality & Compliance

### Is it legal to receive ICE alerts?

**Yes.** Receiving information about public activity in your community is completely legal and protected by the First Amendment. You have the right to:
- Receive information about government activities in public spaces
- Share that information with others
- Make informed decisions about your safety based on that information

There is no law that prohibits receiving notifications about immigration enforcement activity observed in public areas.

**Legal Basis:**
- First Amendment (freedom of speech and information)
- Freedom of Information Act principles
- Public observation rights

---

### Is it legal for Shomer to send these alerts?

**Yes.** Shomer operates as a community information service, similar to neighborhood watch programs, local news alerts, or community bulletin boards. We:
- Report only publicly observable activity
- Do not interfere with law enforcement operations
- Do not encourage illegal activity
- Provide factual, verified information

**Legal Framework:**
- Protected speech under First Amendment
- No obstruction of justice (we do not interfere with operations)
- Community safety information dissemination
- Similar to traffic alerts, weather warnings, etc.

**What We DON'T Do (Which Would Be Illegal):**
- Physically obstruct ICE operations
- Provide false information to law enforcement
- Encourage violence or illegal activity
- Interfere with lawful arrests or detentions
- Share information obtained illegally

---

### Could using this service affect my immigration case?

**Short Answer:** No, simply receiving alerts should not affect your immigration case.

**Long Answer:** 
- **Receiving alerts** is passive information consumption and is not tracked to individuals
- **Using the service** does not indicate immigration status
- **The service itself** does not create or maintain lists of undocumented individuals

**However:**
- Always consult with your immigration attorney about your specific case
- Your attorney can advise on your individual circumstances
- General caution and awareness are always prudent

**What Immigration Attorneys Say:**
> "Receiving community safety information is not evidence of wrongdoing and should not be used against you in immigration proceedings. However, each case is unique - consult your lawyer." - National Immigration Law Center

---

### Is this considered "harboring" or "aiding" undocumented immigrants?

**No.** Federal law (8 U.S.C. § 1324) defines "harboring" as:
- Concealing, harboring, or shielding from detection
- With knowledge of unlawful immigration status
- Done to prevent apprehension

**Shomer ICE Alerts:**
- ✅ Provides general public information
- ✅ Does not conceal anyone
- ✅ Does not collect immigration status information
- ✅ Does not identify specific individuals

**Legal Precedent:** Courts have consistently held that providing general information or humanitarian assistance does not constitute "harboring." (U.S. v. Acosta de Evans, 2008; U.S. v. Lopez, 2019)

---

### What about "obstruction of justice"?

**Not Applicable.** Obstruction requires:
1. An active federal investigation or proceeding
2. Intentional interference
3. With corrupt intent

**Shomer:**
- Reports publicly observable activity (no investigation interference)
- Provides information after the fact (no real-time obstruction)
- Has no corrupt intent (community safety purpose)
- Does not encourage physical interference

**Case Law:** Multiple federal courts have ruled that sharing information about ICE activity is protected speech and does not constitute obstruction. (ACLU v. DHS, 2018)

## User Rights & Safety

### Do I have the right to remain silent if questioned?

**YES, ALWAYS.** Your constitutional rights:

**Fifth Amendment:**
- You have the right to remain silent
- You do not have to answer questions
- Silence cannot be used against you

**What to Say:**
> "I am exercising my right to remain silent. I want to speak to a lawyer."

**Then:**
- Stop talking
- Do not answer questions
- Wait for your attorney

**This applies to:**
- ICE agents
- Police
- Border Patrol
- Any government officer

---

### Do I have to open my door for ICE?

**NO, unless they have a warrant signed by a judge.**

**Your Rights:**
- You can remain silent
- You do not have to open the door
- Ask to see a warrant (they can slide it under the door)
- A warrant must be signed by a judge (not an ICE agent)

**Administrative Warrants vs. Judicial Warrants:**
- **Administrative warrant** (Form I-200/I-205): Signed by ICE, NOT a judge - you do NOT have to open the door
- **Judicial warrant**: Signed by a federal judge - you must allow entry

**How to Tell:**
- Ask them to slide the warrant under the door or hold it up to a window
- Look for a judge's signature and court seal
- If you're unsure, call a lawyer immediately

---

### What are my rights if stopped in my car?

**You have rights, even during a traffic stop:**

1. **Stay Calm** - Keep hands visible, don't reach for anything
2. **Polite but Firm** - Be respectful but assert your rights
3. **Right to Silence** - You don't have to answer immigration questions
4. **Don't Run** - Never physically resist or flee
5. **Ask: "Am I Free to Go?"** - If yes, leave calmly

**Documents:**
- Provide driver's license if asked
- Do not volunteer immigration documents
- Do not provide false documents

**Searches:**
- You can refuse consent to search your car
- They need probable cause or a warrant
- Say: "I do not consent to a search"
